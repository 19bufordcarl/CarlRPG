# Interaction

## Interaction

In combat, you can alter someone’s actions for a round. In an interaction, you can convince them to take actions on much longer timelines.


### Goal

During an interaction, the characters attempt to sway an NPC. They might ask for a favor, try to change the NPC’s mind about a topic, or otherwise convince them of something.

Each NPC has one or more traits. The characters must satisfy each of the traits that cause an NPC to oppose them.


### Time and Order of Play

During an interaction, time passes at the DM’s discretion. The PCs act in the order they

wish. NPCs act when the DM judges it appropriate.


### Traits

Traits guide how an NPC acts. They reflect their beliefs, goals, and passions. Each NPC has from one to five traits.


#### Description

A trait can range from a single word, such as “Sadistic”, to a phrase or short description, such as “Willing to die to destroy the Iron Empire.”


#### Rating

Each trait has a bonus associated with it, from +2 and up.

Rating Description +2 Secondary belief or goal, consistent but malleable trait +4 Core belief, strong trait occasionally bent

+8 Central tenet of existence, character-defining trait


#### Influencing Others

When the PCs attempt to sway someone’s beliefs or convince them to do something outside of combat, consider the NPC’s traits. If any of them would cause the NPC to oppose the request, resolve that resistance with an opposed roll, with the NPC using the trait’s bonus and the PC using a relevant skill.

If the PCs succeed, they satisfy the trait. If they fail, the NPC might offer a price they need to pay to satisfy that trait.


#### Active Trait

An NPC can challenge the characters with one of their traits.

The NPC challenges the characters, demanding they

answer a question or putting forth an offer. The NPC rolls an opposed check using their trait, and the relevant character opposes that roll as normal.

The NPC can challenge the entire group. The NPC rolls, and each PC makes an opposed check against their result. The PCs succeed if half or more of them succeed.

In narrative terms, the NPC may ask the characters a question, demand that they explain themselves, or require a display of power or talent. If the characters succeed, they satisfy the trait. If they fail, they cannot try to satisfy that trait. The NPC might give the characters a price or task to satisfy it.


#### Determining Traits

A Wisdom check can determine one of an NPC’s traits. Grant disadvantage for

hidden ones, or advantage for obvious ones. Some traits are too well hidden and can be impossible to determine in this manner at the DM’s discretion.


### Resolving Interactions

Once the PCs have attempted to satisfy each opposing trait, the NPC’s mind is set. Each trait has been satisfied or has prompted the NPC to make a counteroffer or set a price for their cooperation.

The PCs can try to argue for a different deal or bring up another issue, but they cannot sway the NPC unless the situation changes significantly.


#### Scope

Any changes to an NPC’s attitudes persist indefinitely, unless subsequent events cause NPC to change their mind once more.
