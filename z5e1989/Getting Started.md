# Getting Started

## Introduction

I had idea that I could not shake from my head. What if the design concepts of 5e were sent back in time to 1989, just in time for the release of AD&D second edition? And what if those design ideas carried with them a bunch of thoughts on how to improve on 5e? Mainly because after using my level and role system for 5e, I can’t go back to standard monsters.

In any case, here’s what you get. Some key design conceits:

- Checks and saves are now always successful on a 10 or higher. If you want to make something more difficult or easier, give disadvantage or advantage. • The core math of the game is 5e level 1 to 5, stretched to cover 10 levels of play. The core is taken from Ruins & Reptiles, an earlier pass at pushing 5e in an OSR direction. • Frankly, I am most pleased with my take on the illusionist class. I always felt that the AD&D version was a weirdly limited take on the magic-user. This version adds some armor and thief skills into the mix. • I made thief, assassin, and ranger skills universal, though most classes roll at +0.

Mike Mearls, July 2, 2026

## Basic Rules

The rules of the game determine what happens when the DM judges that even a well-trained person might fail an attempt. In this case, you roll the dice to determine what happens.

You roll a 20-sided die, add and subtract modifiers based on the type of roll, and compare the total to a target number.

If the total of the roll is greater than or equal to the target number, you succeed. Otherwise, something goes wrong.


### The Six Abilities

The six abilities define what a character can do. They each have a modifier. Negative modifiers reflect an area of weakness, while positive ones represent a strength. A modifier of +0 represents an average person.

Strength measures physical might and power.

Dexterity measures agility, coordination, reflexes, and balance.

Constitution gauges health and durability.

Intelligence reflects logical reasoning, memory, and knowledge.

Wisdom measures intuition, insight, perception, and willpower.

Charisma reflects personal magnetism and charm.


### Advantage and Disadvantage

Advantage reflects an edge, while disadvantage represents a drawback.

Roll Two d20s. When you have advantage or disadvantage on a roll, you roll a second d20. Use the higher of the two die rolls if you have advantage, or the lower of the two if you have disadvantage.

Multiple Advantages or Disadvantages. It doesn’t matter how many effects grant advantage or disadvantage. You only ever roll one additional die.

Applying Advantage and Disadvantage. If a roll has advantage and disadvantage, it gains neither. Make a normal roll. It does not matter how many effects apply either of them.


### Ability Checks

You make an ability check when you attempt a risky action. You roll a d20 + the relevant ability modifier. You succeed if your roll is a 10 or higher.


#### Group Checks

Group checks are a special type of check that represents how well a group of people completes a task. Everyone involved in the group check makes the same check. If half or more of them succeed, the group check succeeds.


#### Opposed Checks

In an opposed check, everyone involved in the check rolls. The highest result succeeds. If anyone ties, the tied parties roll again until only one winner remains.


### Skills

Skills are areas of special training. Any character can attempt to use a skill, but only certain classes grant any expertise with them.

Skills have modifiers just like abilities. If a skill applies in a situation, its modifier overrides your abilities. Use the skill instead.

Most characters have a +0 modifier in a skill.

Climb Walls. This skill allows you to climb vertical surfaces.

Disguise. Use this skill to mask your identity or pose as another person.

Find/Remove Traps. Your understanding of mechanical devices allows you to identify and neutralize them.

Hear Noise. This skill measures the keenness of your hearing.

Hide in Shadows. Anyone can hide behind a large rock. This skill allows you to remain hidden in shadowy areas.

Move Silently. Moving silently through a dungeon allows you to escape detection.

Open Locks. With a set of thieves’ tools, this skill allows you to pick locks.

Pick Pockets. With careful timing and a light touch, you can relieve others of their coins.

Read Languages. This skill reflects a knowledge of cyphers and dead languages.

Tracking. You can spot the signs of a creature’s passage.


#### Using Skills

When calling for a check, the DM should first check to see if a skill would apply. If not, then use the ability that best fits the situation.


### Saving Throws

A saving throw, often called a save, represents an attempt to evade danger. It is a check you make in reaction to danger. Some spells and effects grant you a bonus on a save.

To make a save, roll 1d20 + the relevant ability modifier. You usually succeed if your total is 10 or higher, but some saving throws are an opposed check as described above. You succeed the saving throw if you succeed the opposed check and fail if you do not.


### Attacks

When you attack, you roll a d20 + your Strength modifier for a melee attack or your Dexterity modifier for a ranged one. Compare the result to the target’s Armor Class (AC). If your total is greater than or equal to the target’s AC, you hit. Otherwise, you miss.

If you attack with a weapon that your class grants you access to, you gain a +2 bonus to the attack roll. This bonus increases to +3 if you are level 7 or higher.

Spellcasters who make attacks with their spells also use that bonus.


### Rounding

When you halve a number always round down.
