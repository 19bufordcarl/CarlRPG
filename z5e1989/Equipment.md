# Equipment

## Equipment

This section provides the rules for equipment that characters can buy.


### Inventory

Your inventory determines how many items you can carry and how easy they are to access during an encounter.


#### Items

Inventory is measured in items. You can carry a maximum number of items equal to 15 + your Strength modifier.

Big Items. Some exceptionally large or heavy items count as multiple items. Multiple characters can help carry a heavy item. You can distribute the item across multiple characters if everyone is close enough to help carry it.

Small Items. Small individual items, such as rings, jewelry, small gems, and similar items do not count toward your inventory. A group of up to 100 such items, including coins, counts as an item.

Equipped. You can have up to two items readied in your hands, or one item that is equal to two items. Items in your hand count toward your total item limit.


#### Encumbrance

If you try carrying too many items, the weight of your gear slows you down.

Encumbered. If you carry items above your limit you are encumbered. While encumbered, on your turn you can either move or use an action. You have disadvantage on all rolls, and attacks against you have advantage. If you try carrying more than double your limit,

you cannot move or use actions in addition to the other penalties listed above.

### Armor and Shields

The armor table below summarizes the protection you can purchase. The table features several headers, starting with the armor’s name.

Category. Every armor and shield falls into a category: light or heavy. Some armor has a capital B next to its category. This armor is exceptionally heavy. You must have a Strength of +1 or higher to wear it. Otherwise, you treat the armor as if your class did not grant you access to it.

AC. When you wear the armor, use the listed calculation to determine your AC.

AC Bonus. Shields give a bonus to AC while you wield them. You can gain the AC bonus from one shield at a time.

Price. The item’s cost.

Inventory. A shield counts as one item and can be wielded in one hand. A suit of light armor counts as two items and a suit of heavy armor counts as four.
