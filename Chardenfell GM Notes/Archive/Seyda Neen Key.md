# Seyda Neen and the Sixth House

## Beginning the Adventure

The journey to Seyda Neen will be swift and uneventful (the DM can cover this with a few words of description). The DM may wish to require a traveling order here, so that players get used to being on their guard.

```boxed
You finally exit the dreaded cave of the red soul sucking gem. This particular cave exits in what those familiar with Chardenfell immediately recognize to be the Bitter Coast. In the distance to the south west, you see the peaks of the Ascadian Isles, and just behind you, the cave mouth is coming just out of major foyada, the remnants of the ancient lava rivers that once dominanted ancient Chardenfell. The bitter coast is hard to navigate. It is a wetland, and in addition to the many willow trees and oak trees that create a thick and low canopy, giant mushrooms called parasols rise up to impressive heights, often towering above the tree line and casting great shadows. After a short period of gathering yourselves and getting your bearings straight, you are graced with incredible luck and spot a silt strider path.
```

```boxed
Being more or less entirely lost, this is by far your best chance at finding civilization from the wilderness. You follow the path of the distinctive circular footmarks of the great beast, hulking animals with 12 long chitinous legs and an equally chitinous shell at the top where its riders and travels can sit. They are the primary means of overland travel throughout Chardenfell and critical to the trade and travel routes throughout much of the heartland where the ashlands and foyadas would otherwise prove impassable, and the coast is too distant to rely on water based means of travel.

After a few hours you spot a much needed stopping point as you grow weary and your stomach hungry. A simple village in the swampy lands of the Bitter Coast appears on the horizon. You spot a simple signpost that says “Seyda Neen” in Dark Elvish. The village seems to be very modestly sized, you’d estimate maybe holding only a few hundred residents. It has a very simple silt strider port of the north end, and generally seems to be set in a very idyllic and fertile plain of the Bitter Coast, and you can see many clearly worked fields of kresh fiber and ash yams, two crops very common in Chardenfell.
```

```boxed
Approaching the village, the first building you come across seems to be a simple farmhouse with a barn attached. A young dark elf woman is grinding herbs, flowers, and mushrooms with a mortar and pestle, but she sees your group and runs into the house at your approach. Shadowy forms are visible behind closed curtains.
```

At this point, the adventure begins with the characters entering along the track at the west edge of the village map.

Most of the buildings in Seyda Neen have not been mapped. If the party chooses to enter one of them, the DM should describe a typical interior. Houses and shops will be one story in height and will have both front and back doors unless otherwise mentioned. Many buildings have porches.

# Key to Seyda Neen

## 1. Pleasant Farmhouse and Barn

```boxed
Approaching the village, the first building you come across seems to be a simple farmhouse with a barn attached. A young dark elf woman is grinding herbs, flowers, and mushrooms with a mortar and pestle, but she sees your group and runs into the house at your approach. Shadowy forms are visible behind closed curtains.
```

The farmer, Llenula Dram, and her husband Adavos Dram will watch strangers suspiciously through the windows while the daughter, Llenada, hides in the kitchen at the rear of the house. If anyone stops, Llenula will do all of the talking. She will be polite, but quiet. She will never allow strangers inside her house. Llenula understands that her town has changed drastically, but she does not know why.

Her barn is well stocked with ash yams, herbs, and kwama eggs. Four Guar are penned in here as well. Beneath the planks in her bedroom floor she and her husband have stored a sack with 85 gold pieces.

## 2. Constable's Quarters

```boxed
This nondescript square wooden building has heavy shutters closed over the windows. The door looks stout, but stands open. No people are visible. Behind this building is another long, low structure.
```

**Cult Members!** Inside is the constable, **Alandro Sul**, CR 1/8 AC/DC 11 HP 9 (7-11) Atk/Prof +3 DPR 3 Atks 1 × 4 (1d6 + 1) He has been a cult member for 8 months. With him are two other dark elves of house Sul who have recently arrived to visit him (Aveno and Avano). The newcomers are recent cult converts.


```statblock
**Avano Sul:** AC 16 - Netch Hide and Shield - HP 15 Atk +3 Atks 1x (1d6 + 1) - Spear
```

If they are hailed, Alandro will do most of the talking. He will try to find out what the characters are doing in Seyda Neen, how long they plan to stay, and so on. He will try to disarm their suspicions and warn them to stay out of trouble. His friends will support him.

Later if these three become aware of the party asking "too many questions" they will attempt to intimidate the party into leaving town.

Grover keeps a sack in his desk containing 30 gold pieces.

## 3. Empty Barracks

The building behind the constable's quarters is a barracks that used to be occupied by guards for the silt striders traveling through the Bitter Coast. The barracks is empty.

## 4. Dairy Farm

```boxed
This house and barn are clean, limewashed wooden buildings. Several guar hang out just outside the barn. Three nix-hounds puppies are playing in the yard of the house stop to gawk at your group. 
```

The nix hounds are not yet old enough to be afraid. They will yip and bark at the group, but generally will not attack them. After not too long, the dairyman **Devalo Graven** will summon the pups inside the barn and come out to greet the party.

He shares the house with his wife Morgan Graven, and his mother-in-law Ethel Sul. He is a kindly man, and if his judgement tells him that the strangers are friendly, he might even invite them in for trama root tea and guar cheese. He is concerned about matters in Seyda Neen, but can offer no explanations. He has 82 gp hidden in a milk can in the barn.

## 5. Weaver's House and Shop

```boxed
A sign with a spinning wheel and a loom hangs outside this new building. Several bales of kresh fiber are stacked on the wide porch. No people are visible.
```

The front part of this building contains a shop. Here **Ulen** and **Uloo Henakul** run their large loom and spinning wheel. Many piles of saltrice, whickwheat, kreshfiber, cloth, and other traditional dark elven clothing materials and tools lie about in a state of confusion. A small bell will ring if the door is opened, bringing the weavers from their home behind the shop. They are both younger elves, and newly married and moved to Seyda Neen, trying to make a go of their first business, and they are both friendly.

If questioned, they will respond that "people are strange hereabouts," but they know of no reason for their neighbors' aloofness. They will welcome the chance to talk to outsiders, even inviting a small group in for a glass of mazte if the time of day is appropriate. If the conversation continues for awhile they will warn strangers that the Gold Kresh Pub is a strange place, to be avoided if at all possible.

## 6. Gold Kresh Pub

```boxed
This is a large wooden inn. It is decorated with carvings of sheaves of kresh fibers along the eaves of the roof. The inn was limewashed at one time, but much of the paint has peeled. A sign picturing a cluster of kresh and a pitcher of mazte hangs over the door. A guar corral and stable, apparently empty, are off to the side.
```

If the characters go in, the DM should turn to the description of the Gold Kresh Pub following the Key to Seyda Neen. A complete map of the inn is also provided.

## 7. Jeweler and Moneychanger

```boxed
A gem-encrusted ring is pictured on a sign outside of this establishment. The doors and windows are open. The building is small, but looks extremely sturdy; the shutters and doors are of heavy wood, reinforced with iron bands.
```

**Fevris Ull**, the jeweler/moneychanger (AC 10; hp 3), tries to make a living from the travelers passing between Hla Oad, Pelagiad, and the rest of the Bitter Coast who require his services. His business has fallen off considerably in the last few years. After dark, his doors and shutters will be locked. Fevris and his over-bearing elderly father (AC 10; hp 8; Strength 16) live here with two human guards:

```statblock
**Erroll Rocktyn:** AC 15 - Gambeson and Shield - HP 26 Atk +3 Atks 1x (1d8 + 1) - Longsword.
```

```statblock
**Wylle Dunn:** AC 15 - Gambeson and Shield - HP 20 Atk +3 Atks 1x (1d6 + 1) - Shortswords and throwing daggers.
```

 *The latter is posing as a fighter, but is actually a cult member waiting for the right moment to betray his employer.*

Fevris is skilled at working with stones and soft metals, and will negotiate a reasonable fee for this work. He will also exchange types of currency for a 5% charge. In a metal box in his office he has 1000 gp spread across a variety of coin denominations plus 30 gems, worth 1,000 gp, 500 gp (x2), 100 gp (x3), 50 gp (x4), and 10 gp (x20). In addition, four works of jewelry are displayed in his shop and locked in the box at night. They are worth 1,400 gp, 800 gp, and 400 gp (x2).

He has several locked chests hidden beneath a secret trapdoor in his bedroom. The first contains 2,400 gp in medium denominations; the second 220 gp in small denominations; and the third 5,000 gp in very large denominations plus five gems each of 1,000 gp, 500 gp, and 100 gp worth. Each of the chests has a poison needle trap in the lock. The jeweler carries the keys on an amulet about his neck at all times. His guards do not know about the chests.

The DM may wish to keep track of the moneychanger's transactions. For example, if the player characters want to exchange gold for gems, and the moneychanger is out of gems, they may have to wait until more gems become available (or go to a larger town).

## 8. Livery Stable

```boxed
This is an exceedingly run-down structure of wood. Faint traces of a former paint job cling to small parts of the walls, but in some places the bare wood has started to rot. A fenced walkway leads from the road to the back of the house, and guar manure is scattered liberally about. A small lizardfolk child, his face covered with dirt, is playing on the front porch. As you approach, he toddles out to the road, smiles, and says "hi!"
```

This is the village stable, as the characters' noses will already have suggested. Much of the ground has been trampled into mud. The front of the dilapidated house is sheltered by a wide porch. All appearances to the contrary, the liveryman is quite well-to-do. He is simply not concerned with presenting a front of wealth and polish.

The liveryman is a lizardfolk named **T'zilothi** (AC 10; hp 5). Her husband **R'val**, and their three small children live here. The husband is timid, but there is a high chance that he will see his son talking to strangers. If so, he will rush out to collect him, staring at the party with fearful eyes as he snatches him up. If a character makes a friendly comment, he will stop, embarrassed, and mutter some explanation about "mealtime." He will then dash into the house with the child.

 T'zilothi is at work in the stables, but her husband will call her after any encounter with strangers, and she will hurry to the house. If the party is still in front when she gets there in two rounds, she will step outside to apologize for her husband's hastiness. "These are strange times, you understand…" Unlike many of the folk of Seyda Neen, she is affable and willing to carry on a conversation. She will, however, keep his guests on the porch.

T'zilothi cannot explain the forces at work in the village, but she is very observant. After some small talk, she will begin to share her knowledge. She is suspicious of the two strangers who moved into the cottage across the road (9) several weeks ago. "They don't seem to be interested in mixing with anybody." She shares the weaver's apprehensions concerning the Gold Kresh Pub, and will recommend the Retching Netch Inn for its superior mazte and friendly atmosphere. After an hour of conversation, she will mention her neighbors at the general store: "They up and disappeared a couple months ago; gone for nearly a fortnight, then the whole family came back. Didn't even leave one of the boys to mind the store!"

Her stable contains 6 light guar, 2 draft guar, 2 nix-oxen, 2 nix-hounds, and 2 small carts. Her life savings (950 gp and a 200 gp gem) is buried in a wooden box in the farthest stall of the stable.

## 9. Small Cottage

```boxed
This small, square building has been freshly white-washed. Heavy curtains hang over the windows, and the door is shut.
```

This cottage is the home of two wood elves.

```statblock
**Dorian:** AC 16 - Chain Shirt and Shield - HP 25 Atk +3 Atks 1x (1d8 + 1) - Longsword / Longbow; magic missile, protection from good and evil, web
```

```statblock
**Llywillan:** AC 15 - Netch Leather - HP 20 Atk +3 Atks 1x (1d8 + 1) - Longsword / Longbow
```

The townsfolk are suspicious of the pair. Only the mayor knows why they are here, since he has asked them to investigate the strange occurrences in Seyda Neen. The wood elves have learned that an evil cult is somehow controlling the minds of certain citizens, including the two priests of the temple (21), the proprietor and regular customers of the Gold Kresh Pub (6), the constable (2), and the blacksmith (15). The wood elves are careful, however, and will not reveal this information unless they are absolutely certain that the party is opposed to the cult. Any wood elves present in the party will cause the pair to feel a natural affinity toward their cousins, but this will in no way lessen their caution. In general, the party will have to engage in combat and capture or slay some of the known cult members to win the trust of these elves.

When Dorian and Llywillan are convinced that the party is an ally, they will share their information. They will not join the group for exploring either the town of the dungeon, preferring to work on their own.

The elves will remain in their cottage during the day, but at night it is 80% likely that they will be out observing either the temple or the Gold Kresh Pub. They have a small metal box in a cupboard. It is stoutly locked, and Llywillan carries the key at the bottom of his quiver. A thief can try to pick the lock at -10%. The box may also be pried open, but this will release a sleep gas that will affect everyone in the cottage (no saving throw) for 3-18 hours. Note that wood elves will be highly resistant to this gas. Roll the time of unconsciousness separately for each character. The gas is inside the chest, hence no attempt to find or remove traps will be successful. Using the key or picking the lock will bypass the trap. The box contains 2055 gp and this note:

```statblock

My purpose now, sadly, is to ask — nay, beg — my courageous comrades to aid my people in a time of dire need, I cannot even describe to you the danger that threatens Seyda Neen, for I know not its true nature. I do know that, unless it can somehow be stopped, this evil will consume my little village and its families, we will vanish without trace into the the dust of history.

A sinister force is at work here, and it is made all the more frightening by the fact that its true nature is concealed in a web of fear and suspicion, I plead with you come to Seyda Neen, lend your skills to revealing this menace, that it may finally be destroyed!

Lady Raven Dres

*Arbiter of Seyda Neen*
```

## 10. Arbiter's Residence

```boxed
This house is set well back from the road, and is partially screened by a pair of medium-sized mushroom tree stalks. This is by far the most imposing house in the village. The walls have been regularly limewashed and the roof gleams with new wooden shingles. A wide porch crosses the entire front of the house, and columns of wood support the overhanging roof. The columns have been carved into leafy patterns by a skilled craftsman.
```

This is the home of the village arbiter and her family. The arbiter is **Lady Raven Dres**, a dark elf **[spy](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/monsters/html/black_flag/spy_bf.html)** CR 1. She keeps her weapons and armor in a bedroom closet. The mayor's cheerful husband and their daughter live here too. The mayor's personal bodyguard is also concealed in the house.

Raven is polite and curious with strangers, and is willing to spend hours conversing. Always, however, she is discreet, seeking information more readily than giving it away. She is an astute leader and has recognized that something is warping the people of her village. She has associated the changes with recent midnight disappearances, noticing that the folks who return are in a somehow altered state. She is convinced that the storekeeper, the smith, the carpenter, and the priests are all members of this secret order. At first, she believed that this might be a result of the soul sickness the temple preached those who lacked devotion to the Tribunal. But, once she realized the priests were corrupted, she has wavered in this belief, and now believes something more sinister and closer to home is at work. She has formed the false hypothesis that the old hermit in the grove west of town is behind the whole thing. Lack of proof has kept her from acting on this assumption, however.

Hiding inside the mayor's house is her old bodyguard, an unlikely companion, a Nord by the name of **Trevor Stoutheart** CR 3 **[veteran](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/monsters/html/black_flag/veteran_bf.html)**. Trevor will stay concealed in a curtained alcove until needed. He is a gruff, uncommunicative old soldier, who will spring from his hiding place in a curtained alcove at the first sign of an overt act against the mayor. (Trevor's interpretation of an "overt act" may be taken very loosely—the sight of a person who might have placed something in the arbiter's mazte, or a hand suspiciously concealed under a table, could bring him charging into the room with a bloodthirsty challenge. The arbiter will be quite embarrassed if this occurs.)

Behind the arbiter's house is her husband's woodcarving workshop, with tools, woodshavings, and some fine pieces of hardwood lying about. He did the carvings on the columns before their house. He is half-finished with a remarkably lifelike bust of his wife. Beneath the floorboards in the shop he stores a small chest, keeping the key in a secret pocket of his tunic. The chest contains 4564 gp in large denominations and three gems worth 1,000 gp apiece.

## 12. Village Store

```boxed
This is a fairly well-kept establishment with an assortment of farm implements and cooking utensils on the wide porch. The door stands open, and a well-painted sign depicting a pot, a plow, a sack, and a lantern hangs over all.
```

**Cult members!** This family of humans was converted about three months ago. The husband, **Theodore Miller**, wife **Melba**, and three adult sons **Ashton, Jack, and Thorsten** (AC 10; hp 5,3,7,6,5) will all fight, if necessary, using Shortswords. The husband and wife will greet customers in the store. The sons will remain screened by a curtain leading to the back porch, aiming cocked crossbows at strangers. If the visitors are hostile or too curious, the sons will step forward and demand that they leave the store. If strangers behave violently, the sons will shoot first and then leap into the room with swords drawn.

The storekeeper and his wife will put on a friendly front with customers, knowing that their sons are backing them up. Mundane items such as cooking pots or sacks of meal will always be available. Most items listed in the Players Handbook can also be found here (75% chance). Weapons and armor will only be found on a 20% chance, rolled separately for each item.

There are a table and several benches in the store. When strangers visit, the storekeeper will invite them to sit and have a glass of wine, a rarity in rural Chardenfell. There is an 80% chance per character that the individual will find it to be the best wine they have ever tasted. If asked about the wine, the storekeeper will say that it comes from the special shipment he gets from the silt strider every so often. He will attempt to get as much information from the party as possible, but will reveal very little about himself or his family. The store's cashbox contains 50 gp in small denominations. His house, 40' behind the store, is very run-down. In a closet in the master bedroom is a locked chest with 108 gp in it. There is a poison needle trap on the chest, while the key is under the mattress. The rest of his valuables have been donated to the cult.

## 14. Carpenter's Shop

```boxed
A wooden board in the shape of a saw hangs before this well-constructed building. The front part of the structure is unwalled, and inside of this breezy area the carpenter is at work. Many tools (saws, hammers, nails, prybars, etc.) are scattered about, and some boards are mounted on sawhorses.
```

**Cult members!** The older carpenter couple **Nevalo Daras** and his wife **Illyna** (AC 10; hp 3,3) are very recent cult initiates; in fact, their adult daughter is currently being held in the temple by the priests! The carpenters serves as a furniture maker as well as a builder, and numerous examples of his handiwork are placed around his shop. Tables, chairs, and desks—all of exquisite craftsmanship—may be seen in a casual inspection. The table that is presently under construction, however, is much more shoddily put together, as are the pair of chairs he has just completed. The latter await a coat of stain, whereas all of the other furniture is finished.

His wife will spy upon strangers through a small hole in the door of the house. She carries a longsword that has been smeared with snake venom (DC 11 or take 1d6 per round until healed) and will attack hysterically (+1 on "to hit" rolls) if her husband appears to be in danger. The deadly venom has suffered somewhat from exposure to the air, so it is weaker than it first was.

The carpenter has designed a clever secret panel in his bedroom wall that will slip aside to allow access to a compartment beyond (treat as a secret door). Here he has hidden a wooden box, elaborately carved with intricate leafy patterns. The box is worth 400 gp intact. Only the carpenter knows which combination of leaves to press or twist to open the box. The carpenter's treasure, soon to be donated to the cult, is 500 gp and a 500 gp piece of jewelry.

## 15. Blacksmith's Shop

```boxed
This is obviously a smithy, though no sign hangs here. The shop part of the building is unwalled, and two brawny lads operate a bellows while a giant of a man hammers thunderously upon a piece of metal that will soon become the blade of a shovel. Soot and smoke abound.
```

**Cult members!** The smith. Fathrys Daras and his wife Eloyna have been members for nearly a year. The man still does his work with the mechanical precision that has allowed him to maintain the quality that he has always been known for. Formerly a short-tempered and irritable man, the charm of the sixth house has unbalanced his mind slightly. There is a 25% chance that the approach of strangers will cause him to fly into a violent rage, threatening them with the wrath of the gods if they do not flee at once. His wife will attempt to restrain him, but if an individual or group continues to approach, the smith will break free and attack.

Even if he does not fly into a rage, the smith is unpleasant and rude in conversation. Any slightly aggressive or faintly insulting comment will probably (66%) cause him to become enraged. His wife will be unable to hold him as he rushes forward. If combat begins she will support her husband. Because of their size and strength, they are formidable foes, though they fight at 0 level. The smith fights with a warhammer if able, or a shortsword otherwise. The wife fights with a shortsword.

Because of their netch leather aprons, they have an AC of 11. All of their valuables have been donated to the cult.

## 16. Battered and Weather Beaten Structure

```boxed
The doors and windows of this large building are boarded up. A sign lies face-down in the weeds before the porch, which may be reached by climbing three broken steps. The roof has several gaping holes, and the general appearance of the place suggests that it has been long abandoned.
```

The sign in the weeds, if looked at, reads in faded paint: Bungler's Bane Tavern. This establishment has been abandoned for nearly a year, following an attempt by the cult to kidnap the proprietor and his family. The attempt was foiled by the courage of several citizens, and a fierce fight followed, during which all of the citizens at the inn, as well as several cult members, were killed. The battle remains a mystery to the rest of the village, and has been dismissed as just another aspect of the mysterious problem besetting Seyda Neen.

The Bungler's Bane is not really abandoned, however. If the party should break in, or discover the secret door in the rear wall, they will find the ground floor in a state of total disarray, left as it was following the fight. A cellar door is visible, hanging on one hinge, and a creaky staircase leads into darkness.

The cellar is a gloomy place and smells musty and rotten. Characters may notice (50% chance, 90% for elves) a foul, swampy stench in the air down here. Most of the cellar is one large room, containing several crates of spoiled food stores and three kegs of mazte (still good). Two doors lead to smaller storage rooms: a fruit cellar and a toolroom.

The former is empty. In the latter awaits the source of the odor — 3 ash zombies! The zombies will have heard the party descend the stairs, and will be hiding to avoid detection. If the door to the toolroom is opened, they will attack savagely.

```statblock

**Corprus Stench.** *Constitution Saving Throw*: DC 12, any creature that starts its turn in a 5-foot Emanation originating from the beast. *Failure:* The target has the Poisoned condition until the start of its next turn. *Success:* The target is immune to this beast's Stench for 24 hours.
```

The toolroom also contains hammers, nails, some long timbers, and a saw.

### 3. Parlor

Beyond the kitchen is a parlor or sitting room with several comfortable chairs, a large fireplace, a solid wooden table, and several shelves holding many varieties of potted plants and an assortment of dice and game sets.

### 4. Bedroom

The Felden's bedroom has a bed with a kresh fiber mattress, a desk, and several bookshelves supporting the business records of the inn. A large, walk-in closet is in the southeast corner, and here are all of the couple's clothes. A board in the floor of the closet is loose; beneath it are netch leather sacks holding 1020 gp spread across all varieties of denominations.

### 5. Outhouse

The structure is simple, but clean.

### 6. Winery

This is Vedran's pride and joy. Fermenting here are some of the finest saltrice grains and mushroom spores grown for many leagues, slowly turning into various brews of mazte of excellent quality. The outer room is a small office with a desk and many records of different batches of mazte, ranging back for nearly 40 years. The large inner room has huge vats of saltrice and mushrooms in various stages of fermentation; two large clay jars, stoppered, at the west end; and two dozen casks of mazte stacked against the north wall. In the center of the room is a huge bin full of freshly picked mushrooms. There is a lone cask of imported sujamma.

### 7. Lavish Guest Room (2 Gp per Night)

This room has a luxurious featherbed, a closet, desk, table with chairs, and an empty trunk with a key in the lock. As with all of the guestrooms, this one is empty. (A traveler through Seyda Neen is a rare creature these days.)

### 8. Storage Closet

In this closet are extra sheets, pillows, and blankets for the bedrooms of the inn.

### 9. Private Guest Room (5 Sp per Night)

This room has a fluffy kresh fiber bed, a desk, and a table with three chairs.

### 10a.-b. Multiple Guest Rooms (1 Sp per Bed, per Night)

Each of these rooms contains several beds. They are otherwise bare of furnishings.

### 11a.-c. Double Guest Rooms (5 Sp per Bed, per Night)

Each of these rooms contains two beds, a desk, and a chair.

### 12. Cellar

This is a large, open room supported by two central columns. Between the columns are many crates containing supplies for the inn; such as platters, mugs, towels, cheap silverware, a saw, an axe, and several spare hinges. More crates along the north and west walls contain stores of food: ash yams, saltrice, whickwheat, mushrooms, herbs, flour, dried and smoked meat, and several kegs of mazte. The outside cellar door is locked and barred when not in use. Many long planks have been piled along the south wall. A box containing a hammer and hundreds of nails sits on top of these boards. There is nothing here of special interest.

## The Temple of Almalexia

**Cult members!** The temple of Almalexia was dedicated to the living goddess when it was constructed from granite blocks brought all the way from the mines in the Balmora Foyada. However, the sixth house's possession of the two priests and their servants has made a mockery of the church's original purpose. Although weekly services are still held to keep up the pretense, the priests are among the cult's most adamant proponents. They play an active role in the deception of unsuspecting victims.

The granite wall around the temple is 20' high. The gates are strong and wooden. Carved into them with precise craftsmanship is an ornate carving of the living goddess Almalexia, portrayed as a beautiful elven woman with elaborate jewelry. The temple building itself is made of solid granite blocks, and is completely windowless.

### 1. Courtyard

During the day, the courtyard is open. A broad path leads to the doors of the temple. The courtyard itself is empty except for a gardener, and its grounds are not well tended. If disturbed, the gardener (a temple servant; AC 10; hp 4) will rudely direct the characters to the main temple, then ignore them.

**After dark:** The gates to the temple are closed and barred from the inside, and two trained nix-hounds are released to roam the courtyard. These will obey any of the five servants who care for them. The servants alternate the duty of patrolling, and two of them will be with the hounds at all times.

```statblock
**Nix-Hounds (2):** AC 12; HP 11; Atk 1x at +4 for (1d6 + 2)
**Servants:** AC 10; HP 5; Atk 1x at +3 for (1d6 + 1)
```

If a fight breaks out, the three servants who are not on patrol will hear it and join their comrades.

### 2. Outbuildings

The largest outbuilding is a kennel and private quarters for the nix-hounds and servants who patrol the grounds at night. Adventurers investigating it will be rudely turned away. The other buildings house general supplies, farming tools, and the like.

### 3. Central Sanctuary

```boxed
This large room has a tile floor decorated with an abstract mosaic pattern in orange, tan, brown, and blue. Several tapestries decorate the walls, all depicting various beautifully rendered dark elves who some of you would recognize as famous saints of the tribunal temple. Five huge tree trunks serve as columns in the room, and a statue of Almalexia, carved from a solid piece of jade, is mounted on a low dais at the north end of the room.

**If Marise Present:** Tending to the statue you see a dark elven priestess. Her outfit is immaculate and she has lots of nice jewelry. One of Almalexia's domains is beauty and this priestess certainly exudes it.
```

If the adventurers are just visiting, they will be met in this area by **Marise Delvi**, a priestess of the temple (see room 9). She will caution them that non-members are not allowed beyond this area. She will answer questions politely and try to find out what the characters are doing in the area without revealing any information of importance. She will not let the characters get too close to her, and if the party is hostile, she will flee and warn the rest of the temple. Alternately, the DM could have the characters meet a temple servant, with similar results.

The statue of Almalexia is worth 5,000 gp, but weighs the equivalent of 10,000 gp.

### 4. Meeting Room

```boxed
This is apparently a larger room, divided by a rich blue drapery just west of the door. The area that you see is empty except for three plain wooden benches. Behind the drapery is a softly carpeted room with three comfortable chairs placed around a low table. A small shelf to the side holds several unopened bottles of Seyda Neen Special mazte, and a half dozen cups.
```

### 5. Dining Hall

This is the room where the priests, monks, and servants all take their meals together. The pretense of goodness is maintained in a handsome display case, which contains examples of some of the finer harvests to come from the Seyda Neen area: a huge ash yam, a plump blisterwart mushroom, a cluster of tall kresh fiber stalks, and a bottle of the local mazte.

### 6. Kitchen

Three medium-sized ovens occupy the south wall, while cupboards take up most of the storage space. A small table is in the center of the room. The cupboards contain various vegetables, and dried meat; as well as pots, pans, platters, cups, and utensils enough to serve large and elaborate meals.

### 7a.-e. Meditation Cells

These were formerly used as places for priests to pray to Almalexia, but that use is long forgotten. Now each contains a only a simple reed prayer mat.

- **a.** This cell is empty except for the reed prayer mat.
- **b.-d.** Each of these cells is occupied by a single evil [monk](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/monsters/html/5.1_srd_(2015_mm)/acolyte.html).
- **e.** The prayer mat in this cell covers a trapdoor to the lower level (area 19).

```statblock
**Evil Monk (3):** AC 10; HP 9; Atk 1x at +2 for (1d6 + 1); Basic spells
```

If the west door to the cell area is opened, all three monks will be ready for combat (no surprise), but they will not fight unless one of the cell doors is opened. When this occurs, the one who has been disturbed will fight in his room, while the other two will spring into the hallway to the aid of their fellow. All three will fight with a quarterstaff. They have no treasure—their cells are bare except for the reed mats on the floors.

### 8. Marise Delvi's Outer Chamber

This room is bare except for three wooden benches. The east door (locked) leads to the quarters of Marise Delvi (room 9).

### 9. Marise Delvi's Quarters

Marise Delvi will be found here during the evening, and 50% of the time during the day. If not here, she will be tending to business in another room on the ground floor of the temple.

```boxed
 A small bed, a trunk, and a desk are the only items in the room.
 
 **If Marise Present:** Writing in a scroll at the desk you see a dark elven priestess. Her outfit is immaculate and she has lots of nice jewelry. One of Almalexia's domains is beauty and this priestess certainly exudes it.
```

The trunk is not locked and contains only a few clothes and, perhaps, her chainmail armor, shield, and mace if she is not wearing it. Under the bed is a trapdoor leading to the lower level of the temple (area 19).

**Marise** is a dark elf who has been a cult member for nearly a year. Treat as a [cult fanatic](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/monsters/html/5.2_srd_(2025_mm)/cultist_fanatic_mm_2024.html). She has an [assortment of potions](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/generators/treasure/index.html) that the party can collect, and a sixth house amulet hidden around her neck that is made out of gold and has two 200 gp rubies in it. The amulet is worth 1,000 gp intact. Marise has a suit of chain mail and a shield in her trunk that she normally wears under her robes. She wields a mace. Originally dedicated to Almalexia, Marise now seeks to undo her former service in the advancement of the cause of the sixth house.

### 10. Hall of Statues

This hall has a gleaming white tile floor and a series of alcoves along the east wall. Each alcove is lined with black velvet to highlight the beautiful golden items there.

All of these statues represent saints of the tribunal temple that were especially favored by Almalexia's blessing. Each contains an amount of gold equal to 500 gp, but the workmanship of the statues makes them worth 5,000 gp each. From north to south, the niches contain depictions of saint Rilms, Seryn, Felms, Meris, Llothis, and Olms.

The fact that the sixth house's minions have let the exquisite items remain in their traditional spots should suggest that it is best not to tamper with them. This is true, since Almalexia's curse will immediately befall anyone who touches one of them. The effect of this curse is similar to that of the spell bestow curse, granting disadvantage to a random ability but is more difficult to remove. Curses from touching more than one item are cumulative, but only the individuals who actually handle the statues are cursed. Thus, if a character disturbs three of the objects, three curses would be received. A **DC 18 charisma saving throw** is allowed for each curse, with those saving instead suffering the penalty only for the remainder of the day. Failure means the curse will be permanent until the character atones for their sins at a tribunal temple attended by a priest of Almalexia.

### 11. Guardroom of the Undead

```boxed
This bare room contains only some wooden benches and 6 statues which release ancestor shadows upon non cultists entering this room.
```

These statues are bound to [shadows](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/monsters/html/5.2_srd_(2025_mm)/shadow_mm_2024.html) which guard this room. If any creature not aligned with the sixth house comes through the door, they will attack, fighting until they are destroyed or the intruders driven off.

```statblock
**Shadow (6):** AC 12; HP 27; Atk 1x at +4 for (1d6 + 2 Necrotic, reduce Strength score by 1d4, the target dies if strength is 0 or less); can turn invisible in darkness as bonus action.
```

### 12. Library

```boxed
This room contains many shelves of books, and two small tables.
```

Most of the volumes deal with mundane aspects of agriculture or religion. The most ornate book in the room is on one of the tables, entitled: *A Most Worshipful Guide to Almalexia*. The huge and richly embossed volume will not fit on any of the shelves. It contains many colorful paintings and was formerly very valuable, but all of the illustrations have been crudely defaced. Some of the pages have rusty brown spots on them.

### 13. Small Meeting Room

```boxed
This chamber contains a small table and two comfortable chairs, with an unopened wine bottle and two clean glasses on a shelf in the corner.
```

As soon as a party enters room 13 or 14, the **scamps** in room 15 will spy upon them through slits in the secret doors. All of the secret doors are one-way only, from 15 into 13 or 14. When the entire party has entered room 14, the nine [scamps](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/monsters/html/5.2_srd_(2025_mm)/goblin_warrior_mm_2024.html) will attack. These obnoxious little daedra have tough skin and are resistant to non-magical weapons.

```statblock
**Scamp (9):** AC 15; HP 10; Atk 1x at +4 for (1d6 + 2 melee or ranged spell); resistant to non-magical weapons.
```

They will attack in groups of three, one trio through each secret door.

### 14. Torture Chamber

```boxed
A rack and a table with heavy straps attached to it occupy the center of the room, and an iron maiden stands in the corner. Several empty pine boxes, about 6' x 2' x 3', are stacked against the west wall.
```

What was formerly a storage room for the ample supplies of saltrice and vegetables that the faithful provided for their priests' disposal has now been outrageously defiled. It is here that Dinor does much of his dirty work. Marise and Ambarys also use the devices as a means of coaxing information from reluctant citizens before they have been converted.

### 15. Scamp Quarters

```boxed
This room is barren, except for nine kresh fiber mats and a low, crudely-built table.
```

The nine scamps who live here were summoned and bound by the cult's members from the realms of Oblivion. They have been ordered to serve as temple guards, out of sight of the citizens of Seyda Neen (see room 13).

### 16. Antechamber

```boxed
The door to this area is unlocked. When it is opened, several small bells attached to it will chime softly. The walls of this chamber are scratched, stained, and covered with strange markings. Loose chips of stone lie scattered on the floor. Aside from the southeast exit, the room appears empty.
```

This is the antechamber to the quarters of **Ambarys Delvi**, originally a priest of the gentle Almalexia. His charming by the sixth house has seriously unbalanced his mind. He has scrawled meaningless gibberish on the walls of this room with soot and colored pigments.

Ambarys will always be around the double corner at position b, watching the party. He will flee if the party appears too strong, but he will otherwise attack as suggested later.

**Ambarys** is a [priest](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/monsters/html/5.2_srd_(2025_mm)/priest_mm_2024.html) and has an [assortment of potions and charged magic items](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/generators/treasure/index.html).

### 17. Ambarys' Chamber

```boxed
This room is decorated in a bizarre fashion. There is a dirty straw pallet on the floor, yet two soft and expensive armchairs rest at a low table. A beautiful robe hangs on the wall above a battered, locked chest, while the floor is strewn with muddy rags. An exquisitely crafted wooden desk has been placed near the northeast corner of the room. At various points around the walls, small iron hooks for hanging tapestries have been set into the stone.
```

There is nothing of value in this room. The rags are barely recognizable as tapestries. At one time, these showed pastoral scenes of the Bitter Dedric. The desktop is covered with insane scribblings in the daedric tongue: "Snake mother…" and "A crocodile has many teeth" are examples of Ambarys's scrawlings. The chest contains only ripped clothing of poor quality, and worthless fragments of ivory that once made up a statue of Almalexia.

The secret door to room 18 used to be masked with a tapestry, and now may be detected at **DC 10**. It is opened by simply pulling a hidden switch on the desk. This door can be barred from the other side. If Ambarys passes through the door and bars it, he will be able to escape through the secret door in room 18 before the party can break in.

Ambarys carries a ring of keys on his belt at all times, including the keys to his chest and the chests in room 18.

### 18. Secret Altar Room

```boxed
This large room is dirty and unkempt, with shards of rock lying around four crude granite statues standing about the room in haphazard locations. In the southeast corner is a polished jade figure of a snake with a female human head that is set apart from the other statues by its obviously superior crafting and material. Near it, a small cage with iron bars holds the listless figure of a dark elf woman clothed in tattered rags. In addition, two brass-bound chests sit next to the east wall.
```

This chamber provides final proof of Ambarys's corruption. The woman, **Sedyni Daras** (AC 10; hp 4), is imprisoned here. She is the daughter of the village carpenters. Her listlessness will vanish with the prospect of rescue. She made the trek to the sixth house's lair with the rest of her family; but some untapped reserve of courage and determination prevented her from succumbing to the charm. She has since been the subject of horrendous magical experimentation to try to figure out why their charm sometimes fails. She has been horribly mistreated over the last several weeks, but her spirit has not been broken. If Ambarys is not yet dead, she will attempt to kill him if she gets the chance.

She has seen the effect of the charm on her family and will not wish to return to them. She understands that they would only betray her, unless the charm can somehow be broken. Sedyni will relate everything that she knows, but under no circumstances will she return to the lair of the sixth house. Sedyni knows that the chests were emptied recently, so the hoard is now quite meager (15 gp).

The obsidian statue is a skillfully carved image of the ascended sleeper, whom Sedyni will name as **The Ascended Sleeper**. Shades of gold, red, and mostly block obsidian have been blended into a masterpiece of evil. Light from torches or lamps will cause the colors to swirl and shimmer in an almost hypnotic pattern. The statue is worth 7,500 gp intact, and weighs that much in gp equivalent. It is impossible to break into smaller parts while retaining any of its value.

The other statues in the room have been chipped from granite, and are as ugly as they are poorly crafted. Ambarys created them himself, and gleefully explained them to his captive; so the lass can identify them as a scrib, two large kwama, and a cliff racer. The fragments of rock are the remnants of a statue that has been shattered. At one time it was a likeness of Almalexia.

Behind the statue of the ascended sleeper is a secret trap door that leads to room 9 on the ground floor level of the temple. Only Ambarys knows about it, and he will use it to escape if hard pressed.

### 19. Hidden Hallway

This hallway connects rooms 7e and 9. It is entered through either of the two hidden trapdoors. The walls, floor, and ceiling are of rough stone.

### 20. Maze

This small maze has been built in a formerly empty storage room. The walls of the maze are studded with iron spikes at odd intervals and heights. Here, the cult members have captured and charmed a small [manticore](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/monsters/html/5.2_srd_(2025_mm)/manticore_mm_2024.html), keeping it in a nearly starved state. It will not attack a person or group making the sign of the cult, but all others are fair game. The manticore waits at the position marked with an "X" on the map, but will always hear the door open and move to investigate. As with most cult members, it has no treasure of its own.

```statblock
**Manticore:** AC 14; HP 40; Attacks 3x at +5 for (1d6+4)
```

### 21. Secret Door

The secret door of the maze is opened by pulling on a low spike in the proper wall section. It leads to a narrow, dirt-filled crawlspace. At the far end is the year-old grave of a courageous temple servant who tried to resist the kidnapping by the cult. The body is very old, and wrapped in a rotting netch leather tunic. Nothing of value may be found here.

### 22. Ash Zombie Tunnels

```boxed
This earthen tunnel has no stone shoring or supporting timbers. Consequently, much fresh dirt lies on the floor.
```

Two sets of earthen stairways descend a total of 80' before the passage forks. Here sits a squat, mushroom-shaped lump about 4' high.

This shrieker has been placed as a watchfungus. The four ash zombies in the room at the north end of the tunnels will certainly hear the shrieker's alarm if the party is careless, and move to attack.

```statblock
**Shrieker:** AC7; MV1"; HD 3; hp 15
```

```statblock
Ash Zombie: CR 1/2 AC/DC 12 HP 22 (17-28) Atk/Prof +4 Atks 1× (1d6 + 2)

**Corprus Stench.** *Constitution Saving Throw*: DC 12, any creature that starts its turn in a 5-foot Emanation originating from the beast. *Failure:* The target has the Poisoned condition until the start of its next turn. *Success:* The target is immune to this beast's Stench for 24 hours.
```

The shaded sections of the tunnel have about 1' of mud collected on the floor. These areas slow walking movement to 1/2 normal speed as **difficult terrain** (applies to ash zombies also). The four zombies will split up, a pair advancing down each tunnel. They will attack any enemies they meet, and if the party has all gone down one tunnel, the two zombies that they do not meet will come around to attack from behind.

In the eastern corridor are two solid wooden doors set into heavy frames that are barred on the outside. They are used occasionally for storing prisoners, but are now both empty.

In the zombie's lair at the end of the tunnels is a small chest buried in the dirt by the north wall (the excavation will be noticed as if it were a secret door). The chest is locked, and the key is buried in the loose dirt at the mouth of the chamber. The treasure contains 120 gp, five [dark stones](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/magic_items/html/level_up_advanced_5e/dark-stone-a5e.html), a [ring of summon bound shield](https://slyflourish.com/5e_artisanal_database/magic_items/html/vault_of_magic/recondite-shield.html), and a store of enchanted dark steel that can be used to craft 1 metal based magic item no more powerful than a +2 weapon.
