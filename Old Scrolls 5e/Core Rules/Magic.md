# Magic

The following rules apply to characters using magic.

## Casting Spells

Each spell description has a series of entries that provide the details needed to cast the spell. The following sections explain each of those entries, which follow a spell's name.

### Casting in Armor

You must have the necessary Strength to wear armor and not tire to cast spells while wearing it. You are otherwise too hampered by the armor for spellcasting.

### Magic Action

You must be able to use the magic action to cast a spell. If you cannot do so, you can't cast a spell.

### Spellcasting Abilities

Each type of spell uses a specific ability modifier to alter its effect. When making an attack with a spell, your attack bonus equals the spellcasting ability modifier + your proficiency modifier. Your spell save DC is 8 + your proficiency bonus + the spellcasting ability modifier.

## Spell Descriptions

Each spell is defined by the following elements.

### Level

Each spell has a level. Your level must be greater than or equal to a spell's level to cast it.

### Category

Each spell is given a category, based on its type. Different classes may have different type breakdowns, and some effects in the game alter how spells function based on their category. A spell available to more than one class may have more than one category.

### Classes

A spell may list classes as keywords. If a class is listed, the spell is on that class's list of spells.

### Casting Time

The spell lists the type of action needed to cast it, usually a magic action.

### Range

A spell's range indicates how far from the spellcaster the spell's effect can originate, and the spell's description specifies which part of the effect is limited by the range. Range applies only when casting a spell. A spell's effects can move beyond the spell's range after it has been cast, such as a summoned creature moving to attack an enemy.

A range usually takes one of the following forms:

- **Distance.** How far away in feet the target can be. This is expected to be loosely adjudicated by the DM rather than precisely measured.
- **Self.** The spell is cast on the spellcaster or emanates from them, as specified in the spell.

### Duration

A spell's duration is the length of time the spell persists after it is cast. A duration typically takes one of the following forms:

- **Concentration.** A duration that requires concentration follows the concentration rules.
- **Instantaneous.** An instantaneous duration means the spell's magic appears only for a moment and then disappears.
- **Time Span.** A duration that provides a time span specifies how long the spell lasts in rounds, minutes, hours, or the like. While a time span spell that you cast is ongoing, you can dismiss it (no action required) on your turn.

### Concentration

Some spells and other effects require concentration to remain active, as specified in their descriptions. You lose concentration, the effect ends. You can maintain concentration for up to 10 minutes.

**Another Concentration Effect.** You lose concentration on an effect the moment you start casting a spell that requires concentration or activate another effect that requires concentration.

## Targets

A typical spell requires the caster to pick one or more targets to be affected by the spell's magic. A spell's description says whether the spell targets creatures, objects, a point of origin for an area of effect, or something else.

**Valid Target.** To target something with a spell, you must have a clear path to it and it cannot be hidden from you.

**Targeting Yourself.** If a spell targets a creature of your choice, you can choose yourself unless it must be specifically a creature other than you.

**Targeting Areas.** Some spells cover an area. As long as you can target any part of an area, you can use a spell that affects it.

### Saving Throws

Many spells specify that a target makes a saving throw to avoid some or all of a spell's effects. The spell specifies the ability that the target uses for the save and what happens on a success or failure. The DC is determined by the class feature, magic item, or other effect that allows you to cast the spell.

### Attack Rolls

Some spells require the caster to make an attack roll to determine whether the spell hits a target. The bonus to your attack roll is determined by the class feature, magic item, or other effect that allows you to cast the spell.

### Combining Spell Effects

The effects of different spells add together while their durations overlap. The effects of the same spell cast multiple times don't combine. If the multiple castings each have a different effect, use the most powerful version of that effect. For instance, a spell might give a random bonus to AC. Use the highest bonus from among the castings of the spell.
