# Equipment

This section provides the rules for equipment that characters can buy.

## Weight

Generally speaking, your total carry weight is not expected to be tracked exactly at the pound granularity. Instead, the number of pounds a character can carry is intended to serve as a reference point for how much stuff they can have on them given their size, strength, and equipment. The DM ultimately determines what can and can't be carried based on the situation.

## Coins

The table below summarizes the values of the standard coins. 100 coins weighs roughly 2 pounds and fit neatly into a small purse or bag.

| Coin                | Gold Piece |
| ------------------- | ---------- |
| Copper Piece (CP)   | 1/100      |
| Silver Piece (SP)   | 1/10       |
| Gold Piece (GP)     | 1          |
| Platinum Piece (PP) | 10         |

The silver coin is the benchmark for the economy. One silver piece is the rough equivalent of one day of standard "peasant" labor assuming a fantasy world with lots of economic and power inequality. The most expensive items will often be quite expensive compared to this figure.

## Inventory

Your inventory determines how many items you can carry and how easy they are to access during an encounter.

## Items

Inventory is measured in pounds. You can carry a maximum number of pounds equal to 50 plus 20 multiplied by your Strength modifier, stated mathematically as (50 + (20 * Strength)).

**Equipped.** You can have up to two items readied in your hands, or one item that requires two hands to hold.

## Encumbrance

If you try carrying too much weight, it slows you down.

**Encumbered.** If you are carrying above your weight limit, you are encumbered. While encumbered, on your turn you can either move or use an action, not both. You have disadvantage on all rolls, and attacks against you have advantage. If you try carrying more than double your limit, you cannot move or use actions in addition to the other penalties listed above.

## Weapon Properties

This section details the different weapon properties.

**Ammo.** This ranged weapon consumes the listed ammo each time you use it to attack. You draw and ready the ammo as part of attacking with it. If you don't have that ammo, you can't attack with that weapon. The prices for these weapons includes 20 pieces of ammo.

**Finesse.** When you make an attack with this weapon, you can use your Dexterity modifier in place of Strength for attack and damage rolls.

**Light.** Some features and abilities specify the use of a light weapon, mainly two-weapon fighting.

**Melee.** This weapon can be used to make melee attacks.

**Ranged.** This weapon can be used to make ranged attacks against targets within 300 feet. You use your Dexterity modifier for ranged attack and damage rolls.

**Reach.** This long weapon adds 5 feet to your reach.

**Thrown.** This weapon can be used to make ranged attacks against targets within 30 feet.

## Armor and Shields

The armor listings below summarize the protection you can purchase. The description features several components, starting with the armor's name. All armor has a combination of benefits and drawbacks depending on its design.

**Strength.** How much Strength you must have to wear the armor without becoming tired after 10 minutes of activity in the armor, granting the dazed condition until the armor is taken off and a short rest is taken.

**Weight.** How much the armor weighs in pounds and thus how tiring it is to wear.

**AC.** When you wear the armor, use the listed calculation to determine your AC

**AC Bonus.** Shields give a bonus to AC while you wield them. Helmets give a bonus to AC while you wear them. You can gain the AC bonus from one shield and one helmet at a time.

**Disadvantage.** While wearing the armor, you have disadvantage on the listed checks and saves.

**Price.** The item's cost.
