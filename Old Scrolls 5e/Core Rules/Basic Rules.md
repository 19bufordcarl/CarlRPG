# Basic Rules

The rules of the game determine what happens when the DM judges that even a well-trained person might fail an attempt. In this case, you roll the dice to determine what happens.

You roll a 20-sided die, add and subtract modifiers based on the type of roll, and compare the total to a target number.

If the total of the roll is greater than or equal to the target number, you succeed. Otherwise, something goes wrong.

## Advantage and Disadvantage

Advantage reflects an edge that gives you a better chance of success, while disadvantage represents a drawback.

**Roll Two d20s.** When you have advantage or disadvantage on a roll, you roll a second d20. Use the higher of the two die rolls if you have advantage, or the lower of the two if you have disadvantage.

**Multiple Advantages or Disadvantages.** It doesn't matter how many effects grant advantage or disadvantage. You only ever roll one additional die.

**Applying Advantage and Disadvantage.** If a roll has advantage and disadvantage, it gains neither. Make a normal roll. It does not matter how many effects apply either of them.

**Rerolls.** Some effects in the game allow you to reroll a d20. If you have advantage or disadvantage, choose and reroll one of the two d20s.

## Ability Checks

You make an ability check when you attempt a risky action.

## Format

A check looks like this:

- Make a Strength (Athletics) check.
- Make a Constitution check.

## Modifiers

The check format tells you which modifiers to use.

**Ability Modifier.** Use the modifier for the ability listed in the check.

**Proficiency.** If the check lists a skill, you add your proficiency to the check if you are proficient with it.

## Target Number: Difficulty Class

The target number for a skill is a difficulty class, abbreviated DC. The rules often include the DC in the check description, like this:

- Make a DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check.
- Make a DC 10 Constitution check.

## Resolution

The rules either give a specific outcome for success or failure, or the DM determines what happens based on the situation.

## Group Checks

Group checks are a special type of check that represents how well a group of people completes a task. Everyone involved in the group check makes the same check. If half or more of them succeed, the group check succeeds.

## Opposed Checks

In an opposed check, everyone involved in the check rolls. The highest result succeeds. If anyone ties, the tied parties roll again until only one winner remains.

## Saving Throws

A saving throw, often called a save, represents an attempt to evade danger. Some saving throws come up much more than others. Dexterity, Constitution, and Wisdom are considered primary saves, they come up the most. Strength, Intelligence, and Charisma are considered secondary saves, they come up less often.

## Format

When the game calls for a saving throw, it refers to an ability, like this: Make a Strength save. Whenever the game refers to a test, it means a d20 roll, so a saving throw or a check.

## Modifiers

The saving throw format tells you which modifiers to use.

**Ability Modifier.** Use the ability listed in the save.

**Proficiency.** Add your proficiency to the save if a feature of your class tells you to.

## Target Number: Difficulty Class

The target number for a saving throw is a difficulty class, abbreviated DC, like this:

- Make a DC 15 Strength save.

## Resolution

An effect such as a spell gives specific outcomes for success and failure. The DM might modify those outcomes based on the situation.

## Death Saves

Death saves are a special type of saving throw that is not tied to an ability. Some rules and class features apply specifically to death saves. Death saves represent a character's physical health. Taking a death save failure represents significant physical damage. Healing failures is difficult. Player characters die when they reach 3 death save failures.

## Attacks

Attacks are described under the rules for combat.

## Rounding

When you halve a number always round down unless explicitly told otherwise.
