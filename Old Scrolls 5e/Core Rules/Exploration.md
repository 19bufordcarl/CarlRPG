# Exploration

An expedition to a dungeon should feel like a foray into a dangerous location, one where a single misstep can spell disaster. Make too much noise and monsters could come pouring from every direction.

*You may note that many commonly specified rules are not specified here. These are rules that are expected to be handled by the DM. They generally have a lot of variance based on the situation and specific adventure context, and thus are best left to the DM's control for this style of game.*

## Vision and Light

Lighting and obscurement both play a role in determining how far you can see.

### Lighting

Here are the lighting levels, from darkest to brightest.

**Darkness:** You can't see into darkness and creatures can hide in areas of darkness.

**Dim Light:** You can see in dim light, but the shadows allow some creatures to hide here.

**Bright Light:** You can see in bright light, and there is too much light to hide.

**Sunlight:** Sunlight is a special case. Some creatures suffer drawbacks when they are in sunlight. Sunlight can be bright or dim. In either case, it triggers effects that depend on sunlight.

**Overlap:** The brightest source of light determines the lighting in an area.

### Sources of Light

Each source of light typically sheds dim light in a broader radius.

| Common Sources | Dim Radius |
| -------------- | ---------- |
| Torch          | 30 feet    |
| Lantern        | 40 feet    |
| Candle         | 10 feet    |

### Obscurement

Fog, steam, smoke, and foliage can hinder vision despite the lighting conditions. Obscurement reduces the distance you can see but does not block attacks or spells.

**Lightly Obscured:** If an area is lightly obscured, you can only see things within 30 feet.

**Heavily Obscured:** If an area is heavily obscured, you can only see things that are within 5 feet.

## Senses

Unless otherwise noted, a creature has access to the five standard senses of smell, taste, touch, sight, hearing. A creature might also have access to a supernatural sense listed below. Each of these senses includes a range, the distance that they can reach.

**Blindsight.** You ignore darkness and obscurement in your area. You treat creatures and objects within 30 feet as if you could see them in dim light. Objects, such as walls, block blindsight.

**Darkvision.** Darkvision allows you to see in the dark, though with some limitations. You treat darkness within 30 feet as dim light. Darkvision does not function while you are in an area of bright light or sunlight. Obscurement blocks it as normal. You cannot perceive color with darkvision, only shades of gray and movement.

## Falling Damage

A character falling into a pit or chasm takes 1d6 bludgeoning damage per 10 feet fallen and lands prone.

## Burning

A burning creature or object takes 1d4 fire damage at the start of each of its turns. As a utilize action, you can extinguish yourself or a creature in melee range.

## Environmental Hazards

Fire, smoke, lava, acid, and other threats can prove deadlier than a monster. The table below summarizes how much damage a hazard inflicts. The damage type should reflect the nature of the hazard.

Some hazards inflict damage and cause failed death saves, reflecting the overwhelming pain and shock they cause.

You can take the damage from a hazard once per round. Once you take it, you can't take it again until your next turn.

| Severity | Damage | Death Saves |
| -------- | ------ | ----------- |
| Minor    | 1d6    | 0           |
| Moderate | 3d6    | 0           |
| High     | 6d6    | 1           |
| Lethal   | 12d6   | 2           |

### Hazard Examples

Minor hazards include everyday dangers, like a campfire, a flask of acid, smoke from a moderate fire, or a pot of boiling water. These are dangerous but rarely life threatening.

Moderate hazards are larger scale and can kill or severely injure the typical person. They include a bonfire, a pool of acid, smoke from a burning building, or immersion in boiling water.

High severity hazards kill the average person. They include brief exposure to lava, poison gases, and full immersion in acid.

Lethal hazards kill even the mightiest adventurers and include immersion in lava and similar dangers.

## Food and Water

If you do not have food and water for a day, make a DC 5 Constitution save. If you fail, you take a failed death save and cannot heal it until you eat and drink.

The DC increases by 2 each day you must make this check. It returns to 5 if you have food and water for a day.
