# Traps & Objects

## Traps

Traps present a constant, lurking threat.

## Detecting Traps

Each trap lists the check and DC needed to detect it.

## Disarming Traps

A trap also lists the skill and DC needed to disarm it. A trap might also specify that a character needs access to a specific part of the trap to disarm it.

## Triggering a Trap

A trap describes what causes it to trigger.

**Targets.** Traps usually target an area or a specific creature that triggered them.

**Death Saves.** Traps are deadly because they catch targets unaware and unleash lethal attacks. Traps can inflict damage, and some especially deadly ones grant failed death saves.

## Creating Traps

The table below summarizes the basic stats or traps for three tiers broken up by level. Adding failed death saves is optional for a trap. It is best used for a trap that relies on surprise, poison, brute force to threaten a character. These failed death saves persist until you finish a long rest.

A condition granted by a trap generally lasts for 3d6 minutes.

The damage listed is for a trap that hits a character once. For a trap that does ongoing damage, such as a room that fills with poison gas, use damage equal to the minimum die roll. The damage is also based on one target. Halve the dice for traps that hit two or more targets.

| Level | DC  | Damage | Death Saves |
| ----- | --- | ------ | ----------- |
| 1 – 4 | 12  | 2d10   | 1           |
| 5 – 8 | 14  | 4d10   | 1           |
| 9+    | 16  | 6d10   | 1           |

## Objects

Sometimes the party may want to try attacking inanimate objects that are incapable of fighting back. Objects can generally be damaged by attacks and by some spells. The DM may allow a creature to break it automatically with an attack or utilize action.

## Damaging Objects

An object has an AC and hit points.

## Armor Class

Objects larger than a size Medium creature do not have an AC. Attacks against then automatically hit. Otherwise, an object's size determines its AC. If you try to attack an object held by a creature, use the creature's AC.

| Item | AC |
|------|----|
| Coin | 20 |
| Dagger | 15 |
| Shield | 10 |

## Hit Points

An object is destroyed when it has 0 hit points. The table below suggests hit points for fragile and resilient objects that are Large or smaller, but remember these are just guidelines for the DM. To track hit points for a Huge or Gargantuan object, divide it into Large or smaller sections, and track each section's hit points separately. The DM determines whether destroying part of an object causes the whole thing to collapse.

| Object Size | Fragile Hit Points | Resilient Hit Points |
|-------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Tiny        | 2                  | 10                   |
| Small       | 5                  | 20                   |
| Medium      | 10                 | 40                   |
| Large       | 20                 | 80                   |

## Saving Throws

Objects use a +0 bonus when rolling saving throws, or the bonus of the creature carrying it, whichever is higher.

## Damage Types

The DM should use logic to determine if a damage type can reasonably affect an object. Psychic and poison damage should not usually damage one.
