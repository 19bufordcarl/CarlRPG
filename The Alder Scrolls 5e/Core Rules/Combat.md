# Combat

When a fight breaks out, the rules keep the action organized and easy to follow.

## Rounds

Each round is enough time for everyone involved in combat to take a turn. A round lasts about 6 seconds.

## Initiative

Initiative determines the order in which everyone acts during an encounter.

## Rolling Initiative

At the start of combat, you make a Dexterity or Intelligence check to determine your initiative, using whichever stat is **lower**. During the action phase of each round, creatures act in order of initiative. The creature with the highest initiative goes first, then the one with the next highest, until everyone has taken a turn.

## Surprise

You might be surprised at the start of combat. You roll initiative as normal. On the first round, the creatures that were not surprised act in initiative order. Then, the creatures that were surprised act in initiative order.

## Tied Initiative

If initiative is tied, the players decide the order in which the tied creatures act. Once they decide, use that order for the rest of the battle.

## Your Turn

On your turn you can do each of the following once in any order:

- Move
- Take an action

## Durations

At the start of your turn, reduce the duration of your ongoing effects you created by 1 round. If this reduces an effect's remaining duration to 0, it ends

## Size

Size determines how much space a creature takes up. If a creature somehow is in a space that it cannot fit into, it has disadvantage on all attacks, checks, and saves and moves at half speed.

Size also determines reach, the distance at which a creature can make melee attacks. The table below lists size and the space a creature takes up. Space gives the length in feet of a cube that represents the space the creature fills.

| Size       | Reach | Space |
| ---------- | ----- | ----- |
| Tiny       | 5     | 2.5   |
| Small      | 5     | 5     |
| Medium     | 5     | 5     |
| Large      | 10    | 10    |
| Huge       | 15    | 15    |
| Gargantuan | 20    | 20    |

## Movement

You can move once during your turn. You can use your action to move, allowing you to move twice. Characters can move 30 feet by default. Movement speed is modified by Dexterity.

| **Dexterity** | Movement Speed |
| :-----------: | :------------: |
|      -1       |    25 feet     |
|       0       |    30 feet     |
|       1       |    30 feet     |
|       2       |    35 feet     |
|       3       |    35 feet     |
|      4+       |    40 feet     |

## Opportunity Attacks

If your movement causes you to leave the reach of a hostile creature, it can make a melee attack against you as an opportunity attack. A creature can make an opportunity attack once per round.

## Movement Modes

Every creature can walk with ease and can attempt to climb, swim, or jump.

**Climb.** You must make a Strength (Athletics) check to climb. The DM chooses the DC. If you succeed, you climb. If you fail, you cannot climb this turn.

**Jump.** Jumping requires a Strength (Athletics) check with a DC determined by the DM.

**Swim.** You must make a Strength (Athletics) check to swim. The DM chooses the DC. If you succeed, you swim. If you fail, you cannot swim this turn.

## Advanced Movement Modes

Only creatures with a special ability can use these forms of movement.

**Burrow.** You can burrow through loose soil, sand, and other material, but not solid objects.

**Climb.** This creature can climb without making Strength (Athletics) checks.

**Fly.** A flying creature can move vertically and horizontally.

**Spider Climb.** A creature with this movement mode can climb horizontally across surfaces such as ceilings.

## Difficult Terrain

Some areas are marked as difficult terrain. You move at half speed in difficult terrain.

## Actions

With an action you can:

- Attack
- Cast a spell (magic action)
- Dash to move a second time
- Dodge to grant disadvantage on attacks against you for 1 round
- Disengage, allowing you to move this turn without provoking opportunity attacks
- Hide from sight
- Influence a creature's attitude
- Use a skill

## Free Actions

A free action is a simple action, something you can do in a moment in addition to the main action you use during your turn.

A free action is any action that does not require a check or any other die roll.

You can use one free action on your turn.

## Attacks

Most attacks require an action.

## Melee Attack

Melee attacks are made against targets in melee range, the distance you can physically reach.

## Ranged Attack

Ranged attacks are made against targets within your attack's range.

## Other Attack Options

### Unarmed Strike

You can make an unarmed strike as an attack action. If you have any melee weapon proficiency you count as proficient for this attack. When you make an unarmed strike, pick one of the following.

- **Punch or Kick.** You make a melee attack. If you hit, you inflict 1d2 + your Strength modifier bludgeoning damage.
- **Shove.** You make an opposed Strength (Athletics) check against the target. If you succeed, you push them back 5 feet.
- **Grapple.** You make an opposed Strength (Athletics) check against the target. If you succeed, you grapple them. Grapple is a condition explained in the conditions section.

### Two-Weapon Fighting

If you wield two weapons you are proficient with, you can use an attack action to attack with both. You make one attack roll. Your damage depends on the weapons you wield.

- **Two Light Weapons.** Roll both weapon's damage die and add your ability modifier to the combined damage roll.
- **One Light Weapon.** Roll both weapon's damage die and add half your ability modifier to the combined damage roll.
- **No Light Weapons.** You attack with disadvantage. You cannot gain advantage with attacks made with that weapon until your next turn. Roll both weapon's damage die, but do not add your ability modifier to the combined damage roll.

**Extra Attacks.** If a class feature or other benefit grants you additional attacks when you use the attack action, you gain those attacks in addition to attacking with both weapons. You can use either weapon for the bonus attacks and apply your ability modifier to damage with the extra attacks as normal.

## Target

You can target something with an attack if the following three points are true:

- The target must be in range.
- A clear, unblocked path large enough to accommodate the attack extends from you to the potential target.
- The target is not hidden from you.

If you do not meet one of those requirements, you cannot target that creature or object.

## If the Target Can't See You

If the target can't see you but you can see it, such as if you are hidden, your attack has advantage.

## Rolling an Attack

An attack is a d20 roll + an ability modifier + your proficiency bonus.

The weapon, spell, or feat specifies which ability modifier to add to your roll.

If you are proficient with the weapon used to attack, add your proficiency bonus to the roll. Otherwise, the spell or feature tells you if you add it.

## Armor Class

The target's armor class (AC) is the target number for the attack roll. If more than one AC applies to a target, such as if it has cover or you cannot see it, use the highest one unless the target decides otherwise.

**Determining Armor Class.** Your AC is 5 + your Dexterity modifier unless you wear armor or have a feature that alters AC.

**AC Modifiers.** Some elements, such as shields, give a bonus to your AC.

**Targets You Can't See.** If you can't see the target but it is not hidden from you, the target uses the higher of AC 20 or its own AC against the attack.

**Cover.** If the target has cover it uses the higher of AC 20 or its own AC. A creature has cover at the DM's discretion.

## Attack Result

If the result of your attack roll is greater than or equal to the target's AC, the attack hits. Roll damage and subtract the result from the target's hit points. Otherwise, the attack misses.

**Critical Hits.** If the die roll is a 20 without modifiers, the attack is a critical hit. On a critical hit, roll your damage twice (all dice and modifiers) and add the results together.

## Use a Skill

As an action, you pick an item you hold in your hand and use it. You can also use one of your abilities along with a skill to attempt something. Describe to the DM what you intend to do. They may ask for a check, might rule the action impossible, or may add some limitation to what you want to do.

The list below provides examples of what you can do. You can always describe what you want to do and the DM can pick a skill and ability to model it.

### Administer a Potion

You can draw a potion and consume it or administer it to a creature in melee range that is friendly or one unable to prevent you from pouring the brew into its mouth.

### Insight: Study a Creature

You can study a creature to learn about its attitude or internal state with a Wisdom (Insight) check. You can learn about its nature or capabilities with an Intelligence (Nature) check.

### Investigate: Inspect an Object

You can inspect a single object or wall near you. You make an Intelligence (Investigation) check, with the DC determined by the DM. If you succeed, you learn some additional details about the object or notice anything hidden within it, such as a secret door.

### Medicine: Stabilize a Creature

You provide medical assistance to a dying creature in melee range. Make a DC 15 Wisdom (Medicine) check. If you succeed, that creature regains 1 hit point.

### Perception: Scan an Area

You can scan a space that is up to 20 feet on each side. You make a Wisdom (Perception) check. Use the Dexterity (Stealth) check result of hidden creatures as the DC. Hidden objects have a DC determined by the DM.

If your check is 20 or higher but you still failed, you know there are creatures or objects hidden there but you do not learn their locations. The creatures remain hidden.
