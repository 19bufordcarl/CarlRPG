# Damage & Healing

Injury and death are frequent threats, as detailed in the following rules.

## Hit Points

Your hit points represent your energy, luck, vigor, and speed. When you lose hit points, an attack or spell causes bruising, scratches, and other light damage that you can shrug off with rest.

Whenever you take damage, subtract it from your hit points. Your hit points can't go below zero or above your maximum.

Hit points are easy to restore, as they represent only minor, surface injuries.

## Damage Rolls

Each weapon, spell, and damaging monster ability specifies the damage it deals. You roll the damage dice, add any modifiers, and deal the damage to your target. If there's a penalty to the damage, it's possible to deal 0 damage but not negative damage.

**Weapon Damage Rolls.** When attacking with a weapon, you add the same ability modifier used for the attack roll to the damage roll.

**Spells and Other Damage Rolls.** A spell or other effect tells you which dice to roll for damage and whether to add any modifiers.

## Damage against Multiple Targets

When you create a damaging effect that forces two or more targets to make saving throws against it at the same time, roll the damage once for all the targets.

## Half Damage

Many saving throw effects deal half damage (round down) to a target when the target succeeds on the saving throw.

## Damage Types

Each instance of damage has a type, like fire or slashing. Damage types are acid, bludgeoning, cold, fire, force, lightning, necrotic, piercing, poison, psychic, radiant, slashing, and thunder.

## Healing

When you regain hit points, add the restored hit points to your current hit points.

## Resistance and Immunity

If you have resistance to a damage type, take half damage (round down) from it. If you have immunity, reduce it to 0.

## Dropping to 0 Hit Points

When a creature drops to 0 hit points, it suffers an actual, lasting wound that might kill it.

## Death

**Monster or NPC Death.** A monster or NPC dies the instant it drops to 0 hit points. Optionally, the DM can instead give the creature the dying condition.

**Hit Point Maximum of 0.** A creature dies if its hit point maximum reaches 0.

**Death Saves.** You die immediately if you gain a third failed death save.

## Dying

If you are reduced to 0 hit points you are dying and must make a death save (described below). While you are dying, you can still act.

**Death Saves.** You make a death save when you take damage that reduces you to 0 hit points.

Roll a d20 to make a death save, a special type of saving throw that does not use an ability modifier.

- If you roll a 20 on the d20, you regain 1 hit point.
- If the roll is 10 or higher, nothing happens.
- If the roll is less than 10, you gain a failed death save.
- If you roll a 1 on the d20, you gain two failed death saves.

**Three Failed Death Saves.** When you gain your third failed death save, you die. If you gain it during your turn, you die at the end of your turn but cannot regain hit points that turn.

**Healing Failed Death Saves.** In order to restore lost death saves, you must seek the aid of a healer, either magical or mundane. The exact healing required depends on the nature of the injury as determined by the DM. Generally, a PC who knows healing magic would be capable of mending the average wound representing a failed death save over the course of an entire day. Think of failed death saves like scars; they are mortal wounds to your character.

**Other Ways to Gain Death Saves.** Some special effects, such as traps, might inflict failed death saves. These effects typically bypass your hit points by striking when you are helpless or otherwise unable to defend yourself.

**Blaze of Glory.** While you are dying, you can choose to go out in a blaze of glory. You die at the end of your turn, but automatically critically succeed on anything you do that turn. If the DM allows it, you can even take actions that would normally not be allowed by the rules provided they are in the spirit of the game. At the end of your turn, you die.
